Introducing the Seven Bodies

A concise spoken introduction for yoga practitioners.

Opening · 0:00–0:30Namaste everyone. In yoga, we begin with the body, but we do not end with the body. Asana makes us aware of physical sensation, pranayama brings us closer to vital energy, and meditation reveals increasingly subtle layers of our experience. Today, let us briefly explore a seven-body model—a spiritual map that helps us understand our journey from the visible body toward deeper consciousness.

1 · Physical BodyThe first is the Physical Body, associated here with Annamaya Kosha—the body sustained by food. It needs nourishment, movement, sunlight, sleep and rest. Our yoga postures help us listen to this body, care for it and prepare it to sit comfortably in stillness.

2 · Etheric BodyThe second is the Etheric or Energy Body, associated with Pranamaya Kosha. It represents the movement of prana through the subtle energy system. Breath awareness and meditation are used to bring balance, vitality and greater sensitivity to this layer.

3 · Astral BodyThe third is the Astral or Emotional Body, associated with Manomaya Kosha. It holds our emotions, desires and attachments. Here, practice means observing anger, fear, jealousy or resentment without being ruled by them, and consciously cultivating love, gratitude, forgiveness and compassion.

4 · Mental BodyThe fourth is the Mental or Thought Body, associated in this sequence with Vijnanamaya Kosha. Every repeated thought leaves an impression. Through meditation, we learn to witness thought, strengthen discernment and replace harmful mental patterns with clarity and benevolent intention.

5 · Causal BodyThe fifth is the Causal Body, associated here with Anandamaya Kosha. This teaching describes it as a storehouse of accumulated experience, wisdom and karma. By learning from life and purifying the earlier layers, we gradually move toward a quieter and deeper sense of being.

6 · Supra-Causal BodyThe sixth is the Supra-Causal or Cosmic Body, associated with Vishwamaya Kosha. The emphasis shifts from “What do I want?” to “How may I serve?” It points toward Aham Brahmasmi—the realization that the same consciousness is present throughout existence.

7 · Nirvanic BodyThe seventh is the Nirvanic Body, associated with Nirvanamaya Kosha. It represents the dissolving of separate identity, complete surrender and liberation. The teaching expresses this state simply as, “I am nothing”—not emptiness in a negative sense, but freedom from the limited ego.

Closing · 3:30–4:00These seven bodies are not anatomical structures; they are a spiritual framework for self-observation. We do not need to force an experience of any higher body. Our work is practical: care for the physical body, balance the breath, purify emotions, observe thoughts, grow in wisdom and serve with compassion. As these qualities deepen, yoga becomes more than something we practise—it becomes the way we live. Pause. Let us carry this awareness into our practice today. Namaste.

Facilitator note: Speak at an unhurried pace and pause briefly after each body. The Kosha pairings follow the course sequence as a teaching aid; the two spiritual systems are related but not exact equivalents.

Physical Body · Annamaya Kosha

Sthula Sharira · biological body

The five-element body needs food, water, air, sunlight, sleep and rest. The speaker calls it an “innocent reflection” of conditions in the subtler bodies.

  • Only this body requires physical nourishment and oxygen.
  • Clean food and adequate rest support its care.
  • The talk recommends avoiding meat and alcohol within its spiritual framework.

Practice: physical care, rest, clean food and meditation.

0223:25–28:39 · 46:20–48:16

Etheric Body · Pranamaya Kosha

Energy body · vital or pranic body

This field is described as carrying prana through 72,000 nadis and seven chakras. Cosmic energy is said to enter through the Brahmarandhra during meditation.

  • Shown as a blue field extending beyond the body.
  • Said to expand with rest and meditation and contract after exertion.
  • The talk places the first signs of illness—and the beginning of healing—at this level.
  • It is said to be released with the physical body at death.

Practice: meditation, appropriate sunlight and clearing energetic blockages.

0328:46–35:13 · 40:02–41:07 · 48:16–49:08

Astral Body · Manomaya Kosha

Emotional body

The astral body carries emotions, desires and attachments and is described as a lighter duplicate of the physical body, joined to it by a silver cord.

  • It is said to travel during sleep for learning, guidance or contact with loved ones.
  • Vivid waking impressions are interpreted as possible astral encounters.
  • After physical death, the soul is said to learn and heal in the astral world before releasing this body.

Practice: transform fear, anger, jealousy and resentment through love, forgiveness, gratitude, acceptance and compassion.

0435:23–37:00 · 41:13–41:44 · 49:09–51:16

Mental Body · Vijnanamaya Kosha

Thought body

This body is formed from thoughts and intentions. Negative thought patterns are said to make it dull or distorted, while constructive thoughts make it bright and colourful.

  • Even a consciously forgotten incident may leave a thought-form behind.
  • Unforgiveness is presented as an obstacle to spiritual movement.
  • In the mental world, the soul is said to review its choices, intentions and treatment of others.

Practice: observe thoughts, release harmful intentions, forgive, introspect and meditate.

0537:00–39:08 · 51:16–55:29

Causal Body · Anandamaya Kosha

Karana Sharira · accumulated experience

The causal body is presented as the soul's continuing individual body, preserving experience, conditioning, wisdom and accumulated karma across incarnations.

  • Its radiance is said to reflect the maturity and wisdom of the soul.
  • The first four bodies are purified to brighten this body.
  • After death, the soul reviews its life and prepares a blueprint for another birth.
  • Relationships and circumstances are described as opportunities for learning.

Practice: learn from experience, resolve karma and cultivate wisdom through conscious living.

0639:17–43:15 · 44:28–45:25

Supra-Causal Body · Vishwamaya Kosha

Mahakarana Sharira · cosmic body

This body is described as dormant until the causal body becomes purified. Its awakening shifts life from personal interest toward a higher purpose and service to the world.

  • Development is gradual rather than immediate.
  • It is associated with Aham Brahmasmi—recognition of one consciousness throughout existence.
  • The talk describes this and the nirvanic body as universal rather than individually separate.

Practice: service, meditation, karmic purification and movement beyond ego-centred identity.

0743:16–46:14

Nirvanic Body · Nirvanamaya Kosha

Liberation · completion of the earthly school

The highest state in the talk is expressed as “I am nothing”: the dissolution of separate identity and freedom from compulsory rebirth.

  • A liberated being may return voluntarily out of compassion.
  • Angulimala, Valmiki and Milarepa are offered as examples of profound transformation.
  • The path is described as long and difficult, but possible.

Practice: complete surrender, non-attachment, compassion, self-transcendence and sustained meditation.

Study note: Kosha names are paired by sequence as a learning aid; the Kosha and seven-body systems are related spiritual frameworks, not exact one-to-one anatomical classifications. These cards summarize Neeta Bhojwani's teachings. Subtle bodies, astral travel, karmic records and energy-based explanations of illness are not established findings in anatomy or medicine.
