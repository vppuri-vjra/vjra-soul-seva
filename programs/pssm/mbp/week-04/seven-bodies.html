Meditation, Body & Energy
for 7 Bodies

Neeta Bhojwani's talk organized into seven clear layers—from care of the physical body to the spiritual model of liberation.

Open each card to study the body's role, the speaker's main teachings, and the practice associated with that layer. Timestamps are approximate because the source is an automatically generated transcript.

0117:41–23:16

Physical Body

Sthula Sharira · biological body

The five-element body needs food, water, air, sunlight, sleep and rest. The speaker calls it an “innocent reflection” of conditions in the subtler bodies.

  • Only this body requires physical nourishment and oxygen.
  • Clean food and adequate rest support its care.
  • The talk recommends avoiding meat and alcohol within its spiritual framework.

Practice: physical care, rest, clean food and meditation.

0223:25–28:39 · 46:20–48:16

Etheric Body

Energy body · vital or pranic body

This field is described as carrying prana through 72,000 nadis and seven chakras. Cosmic energy is said to enter through the Brahmarandhra during meditation.

  • Shown as a blue field extending beyond the body.
  • Said to expand with rest and meditation and contract after exertion.
  • The talk places the first signs of illness—and the beginning of healing—at this level.
  • It is said to be released with the physical body at death.

Practice: meditation, appropriate sunlight and clearing energetic blockages.

0328:46–35:13 · 40:02–41:07 · 48:16–49:08

Astral Body

Emotional body

The astral body carries emotions, desires and attachments and is described as a lighter duplicate of the physical body, joined to it by a silver cord.

  • It is said to travel during sleep for learning, guidance or contact with loved ones.
  • Vivid waking impressions are interpreted as possible astral encounters.
  • After physical death, the soul is said to learn and heal in the astral world before releasing this body.

Practice: transform fear, anger, jealousy and resentment through love, forgiveness, gratitude, acceptance and compassion.

0435:23–37:00 · 41:13–41:44 · 49:09–51:16

Mental Body

Thought body

This body is formed from thoughts and intentions. Negative thought patterns are said to make it dull or distorted, while constructive thoughts make it bright and colourful.

  • Even a consciously forgotten incident may leave a thought-form behind.
  • Unforgiveness is presented as an obstacle to spiritual movement.
  • In the mental world, the soul is said to review its choices, intentions and treatment of others.

Practice: observe thoughts, release harmful intentions, forgive, introspect and meditate.

0537:00–39:08 · 51:16–55:29

Causal Body

Karana Sharira · accumulated experience

The causal body is presented as the soul's continuing individual body, preserving experience, conditioning, wisdom and accumulated karma across incarnations.

  • Its radiance is said to reflect the maturity and wisdom of the soul.
  • The first four bodies are purified to brighten this body.
  • After death, the soul reviews its life and prepares a blueprint for another birth.
  • Relationships and circumstances are described as opportunities for learning.

Practice: learn from experience, resolve karma and cultivate wisdom through conscious living.

0639:17–43:15 · 44:28–45:25

Supra-Causal Body

Mahakarana Sharira · cosmic body

This body is described as dormant until the causal body becomes purified. Its awakening shifts life from personal interest toward a higher purpose and service to the world.

  • Development is gradual rather than immediate.
  • It is associated with Aham Brahmasmi—recognition of one consciousness throughout existence.
  • The talk describes this and the nirvanic body as universal rather than individually separate.

Practice: service, meditation, karmic purification and movement beyond ego-centred identity.

0743:16–46:14

Nirvanic Body

Liberation · completion of the earthly school

The highest state in the talk is expressed as “I am nothing”: the dissolution of separate identity and freedom from compulsory rebirth.

  • A liberated being may return voluntarily out of compassion.
  • Angulimala, Valmiki and Milarepa are offered as examples of profound transformation.
  • The path is described as long and difficult, but possible.

Practice: complete surrender, non-attachment, compassion, self-transcendence and sustained meditation.

Study note: These cards summarize Neeta Bhojwani's spiritual teachings. Subtle bodies, astral travel, karmic records and energy-based explanations of illness are not established findings in anatomy or medicine.
