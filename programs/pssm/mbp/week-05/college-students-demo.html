Session Overview · Seven Sections

15-Minute Session Journey

Teachable Point of View: From Reaction to Choice — Awareness of the thinking–feeling cycle, followed by repeated constructive action, can help students build more adaptive patterns.

Audience: College students

Application: Academic pressure, feedback, relationships, career uncertainty and everyday choices

01 · Interactive Opening · 0:00–2:00

Think Like an Innovator

Facilitator: “How many of you would like to create something meaningful—perhaps become an innovator like Steve Jobs or Elon Musk, or make an impact in your own unique way?”

Invite students to raise their hands. “Now imagine that you present an exciting idea for an app, business, research project or social initiative—and someone says, ‘This idea is not ready.’”

“One student may think, ‘I am not capable,’ feel discouraged and stop. Another may ask, ‘What can I learn and improve?’ feel curious and continue.”

“The feedback is the same, but the thinking–feeling cycle can create two different directions. Awareness gives us a place to choose.”

02 · Mind Experiment · 2:00–4:00

One Message, Four Movies

Display this message without explanation: “Can you meet me after class?”

Ask students to hold up one, two, three or four fingers for the movie their mind created:

  1. Trouble: “I have done something wrong.”
  2. Opportunity: “Maybe I am being invited to something.”
  3. Mystery: “I need more information.”
  4. Neutral: “It is simply a meeting request.”

Ask: “What feeling did your movie create in your body?” Then reveal: the message was the fact; the movie was the interpretation.

“One message entered the room, but many emotional movies appeared. The mind often writes a story before it has all the facts.”

03 · Core Concept · 4:00–6:00

The Thinking–Feeling–Thinking Cycle

A thought can influence emotion and bodily sensation. That feeling can then influence the next thought, impulse and action.

Message → “I am in trouble” → Anxiety → More worried thoughts → Avoidance

The thought is not the same as the fact. Awareness helps a student separate what happened from the meaning the mind automatically attached to it.

Science note: Stress is a brain-and-body response to challenge. It can change alertness and bodily sensations, but no single brain chemical creates a particular thought or feeling.

04 · Creative Activity · 6:00–10:00

Director’s Cut: Rewrite the Next Scene

Relatable scene: An important assignment is due tomorrow. You open the laptop, see how much work remains and immediately feel overwhelmed.

Automatic Scene

“I cannot finish this.”

Anxiety rises. The student reaches for the phone, scrolls and creates even more pressure.

→

Director’s Cut

“Cut. What is the smallest useful step I can complete now?”

Silence the phone, set a 20-minute timer and outline the first section.

Your turn: On one sticky note, write a real student-life trigger and its automatic next scene. On a second note, write “Director’s Cut” and script one constructive action you can take within 24 hours.

Pair rehearsal: Partner A reads the automatic scene. Partner B says, “Cut!” Partner A then reads the new scene and names the next action. Switch roles.

“We are not deleting the feeling or pretending everything is positive. We are directing the next action with awareness.”

05 · Learning & the Brain · 10:00–12:00

Neuroplasticity: Rehearsing a New Pattern

Neuroplasticity is the brain’s capacity to change its connections and patterns through experience and learning. One different response is useful; a more reliable pattern develops through repeated practice.

Trigger → Pause → Name the thought and feeling → Check the facts → Choose one next step → Learn

Neuroplasticity does not mean every outcome is under our control. It means our responses and skills can continue to develop.

Science references: NIMH · I’m So Stressed Out! · NIH · How Short Breaks Help the Brain Learn New Skills

06 · Awareness Experiment · 12:00–14:00

The Seven-Day Replay

For seven days, choose one recurring trigger—opening an assignment, receiving feedback, comparing yourself online or preparing for an interview. Complete one 60-second replay:

  1. Pause: Stop before acting on the first impulse.
  2. Name: “The fact is ___. My thought is ___. I feel ___.”
  3. Direct: Say “Cut,” then choose one small useful action.
  4. Record: Write one line about what happened next.
Awareness → Choice → Repetition → A more adaptive pattern

07 · Reflection & Close · 14:00–15:00

What Will You Practise?

Key message:

  • Thoughts and feelings influence what students do next.
  • Repeated reactions can become habits, while repeated conscious responses can build adaptability.
  • Awareness does not remove every challenge; it creates a moment in which a more useful response becomes possible.

Closing question: “The next time pressure arrives, what new scene do you want to rehearse?”
