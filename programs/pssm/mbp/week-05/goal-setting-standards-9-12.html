Standards 9–12 · Interactive Session · 10 Minutes

Goal GPS

Set the destination. Take the first step. Learn and keep moving.

Teachable Point of View: A goal gives direction to our attention and actions. Possibility thinking helps us begin; purposeful action and practice create progress.

Audience: Students in Standards 9–12

Source: MBTP Module 3, with special reference to pages 18–19

1 · Imagine2 · Understand3 · Create4 · Check5 · Commit

01 · Interactive Opening · 0:00–1:30

Where Should the Driver Go?

Facilitator: “Imagine getting into a cab. The driver asks, ‘Where would you like to go?’ You reply, ‘Anywhere is fine—just keep driving.’ What will happen?”

Invite two or three responses.

“You may move, but you may not reach anywhere meaningful. A goal works like a destination on a map. It gives direction to our time, attention and actions.”

Ask for a show of hands: “How many of you have wanted better marks, improved fitness, a new skill or greater confidence—but did not know where to begin?”

“Today, we will turn one wish into a destination and one first step.”

02 · Module 3 Connection · 1:30–3:00

Direction Changes the Journey

“Module 3 tells us that setting a goal gives specific direction to life. Without direction, we can drift from one distraction to another.”

Compare these responses:

  • “This is too difficult. I cannot do it.”
  • “This is difficult, but I can learn. What should I do first?”

“The second thought does not guarantee success. It creates a more useful starting point for effort, learning and improvement.”

Goal = Direction   ·   Action = Movement   ·   Practice = Progress

03 · Student Activity · 3:00–6:30

Create Your Goal GPS

Ask each student to choose one meaningful goal related to studies, health, relationships, creativity, service or a future career. Divide a page into four parts:

1 · Destination

What exactly do I want to achieve?

Write one clear, observable outcome.

2 · Purpose

Why does this matter to me?

Complete: “This matters to me because…”

3 · First Step

What can I do within 24 hours?

Choose one small and useful action.

4 · Reroute

What will I do when an obstacle appears?

Complete: “If ___ happens, then I will ___.”

Student Example

Wish: “I want to improve in mathematics.”

Goal: “I want to score at least 80% in my next mathematics assessment.”

First step: “Tomorrow at 6:00 p.m., I will revise one chapter for 20 minutes and mark the questions I do not understand.”

Reroute: “If I want to check my phone, I will place it outside the room until the 20 minutes are complete.”

04 · Pair Check · 6:30–8:30

Is the Route Clear?

Students share their Goal GPS with a partner. The partner asks only:

  1. Is the destination clear?
  2. Can the first step be completed within 24 hours?
  3. What obstacle are you preparing for?

Give each partner 45 seconds, then switch.

Facilitator: “Underline your first step and place a star beside the exact time you will do it. A goal without a next action remains a wish. A goal with a scheduled first step becomes a plan.”

05 · Commitment & Close · 8:30–10:00

What Will You Do Today?

Invite two students to complete:

“My goal is ___, and my first step is ___.”

“Your goal gives you direction, but you still have to walk the path. Do not wait until you feel completely confident. Confidence often develops after we begin, learn and improve.”

  • Choose a meaningful destination.
  • Believe that growth is possible.
  • Take one clear step.
  • Review, learn and continue.

Closing question: “What step will you take today toward becoming the person you want to be?”
