How the Brain Works

The brain is a highly connected organ whose neurons communicate through electrical and chemical signals. This page organizes the principal regions and shows how thinking, emotion, memory and bodily regulation work through connected networks.

Brain Systems Diagram & Reference Table
How to read this map: “Thinking brain,” “feeling brain” and “primitive brain” are teaching shortcuts. The structures do not operate as three separate brains; interconnected networks work together in every thought, feeling and action.
CategoryPartFunction
Think & DecideNeocortexSupports perception, language, learning, abstract thinking and conscious awareness.
Think & DecideFrontal LobeSupports planning, attention, voluntary action and socially appropriate behaviour.
Think & DecidePrefrontal CortexHelps with judgment, decision-making, priorities, self-control and problem-solving.
ConnectionCorpus CallosumConnects the left and right hemispheres so they can exchange information.
Feel, Remember & InterpretThalamusRelays and coordinates most sensory information before it reaches the cortex.
Feel, Remember & InterpretAmygdalaDetects emotionally significant information and supports emotional and threat learning.
Feel, Remember & InterpretHippocampusForms new memories and provides context for present experiences.
Feel, Remember & InterpretHypothalamusRegulates stress, hunger, temperature, sleep, hormones and automatic body responses.
Hormonal RegulationPituitary GlandReleases hormones that regulate growth, stress, reproduction and other body functions.
Sleep–Wake TimingPineal GlandProduces melatonin, which helps communicate the timing of the sleep–wake cycle.
Motivation & MovementVentral Tegmental Area (VTA)Sends dopamine signals involved in motivation, reinforcement learning and attention.
Motivation & MovementSubstantia NigraProduces dopamine that helps regulate movement and action selection.
Motivation & MovementStriatumSupports movement, habit learning, motivation and the selection of actions.
Regulate & ActMidbrainSupports alertness, movement, orientation and important dopamine pathways.
Regulate & ActPonsHelps regulate sleep, breathing, facial functions and communication with the cerebellum.
Regulate & ActReticular FormationHelps maintain wakefulness, attention and basic automatic functions.
Regulate & ActCerebellumCoordinates movement, balance, timing and aspects of learning.
Brain–Body ConnectionSpinal CordCarries sensory, motor and automatic-control signals between the brain and body.
Automatic Body ControlAutonomic Nervous SystemRegulates involuntary functions such as heart rate, breathing, pupils and digestion.
Automatic Body ControlSympathetic SystemMobilizes the body for challenge through fight, flight or freeze responses.
Automatic Body ControlParasympathetic SystemSupports rest, digestion, recovery and energy conservation.
Stress PathwayAdrenal GlandsRelease hormones such as cortisol and adrenaline during a stress response.
Reference note: The adrenal glands and autonomic nervous system are included to complete the brain–body pathways, although they are not individual brain parts.
