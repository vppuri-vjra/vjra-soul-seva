How the Brain Works

The brain is a highly connected organ whose neurons communicate through electrical and chemical signals. This page organizes the principal regions and shows how thinking, emotion, memory and bodily regulation work through connected networks.

Brain Systems Diagram & Reference Table
How to read this map: “Thinking brain,” “feeling brain” and “primitive brain” are teaching shortcuts. The structures do not operate as three separate brains; interconnected networks work together in every thought, feeling and action.

Think & Decide · 3 parts
PartFunction
NeocortexSupports perception, language, learning, abstract thinking and conscious awareness.
Frontal LobeSupports planning, attention, voluntary action and socially appropriate behaviour.
Prefrontal CortexHelps with judgment, decision-making, priorities, self-control and problem-solving.
Connection · 1 part
PartFunction
Corpus CallosumConnects the left and right hemispheres so they can exchange information.
Feel, Remember & Interpret · 4 parts
PartFunction
ThalamusRelays and coordinates most sensory information before it reaches the cortex.
AmygdalaDetects emotionally significant information and supports emotional and threat learning.
HippocampusForms new memories and provides context for present experiences.
HypothalamusRegulates stress, hunger, temperature, sleep, hormones and automatic body responses.
Hormonal Regulation · 1 part
PartFunction
Pituitary GlandReleases hormones that regulate growth, stress, reproduction and other body functions.
Sleep–Wake Timing · 1 part
PartFunction
Pineal GlandProduces melatonin, which helps communicate the timing of the sleep–wake cycle.
Motivation & Movement · 3 parts
PartFunction
Ventral Tegmental Area (VTA)Sends dopamine signals involved in motivation, reinforcement learning and attention.
Substantia NigraProduces dopamine that helps regulate movement and action selection.
StriatumSupports movement, habit learning, motivation and the selection of actions.
Regulate & Act · 4 parts
PartFunction
MidbrainSupports alertness, movement, orientation and important dopamine pathways.
PonsHelps regulate sleep, breathing, facial functions and communication with the cerebellum.
Reticular FormationHelps maintain wakefulness, attention and basic automatic functions.
CerebellumCoordinates movement, balance, timing and aspects of learning.
Brain–Body Connection · 1 part
PartFunction
Spinal CordCarries sensory, motor and automatic-control signals between the brain and body.
Automatic Body Control · 3 parts
PartFunction
Autonomic Nervous SystemRegulates involuntary functions such as heart rate, breathing, pupils and digestion.
Sympathetic SystemMobilizes the body for challenge through fight, flight or freeze responses.
Parasympathetic SystemSupports rest, digestion, recovery and energy conservation.
Stress Pathway · 1 part
PartFunction
Adrenal GlandsRelease hormones such as cortisol and adrenaline during a stress response.
Reference note: The adrenal glands and autonomic nervous system are included to complete the brain–body pathways, although they are not individual brain parts.
