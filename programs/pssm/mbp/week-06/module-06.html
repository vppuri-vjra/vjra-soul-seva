01

Five Finger Concept

Pages 2–4

Patriji uses the five fingers as a teaching model for the physical body, mind, intelligence, individual self and cosmic self. Each finger is distinct, yet all belong to one hand.

Little fingerPhysical body
Ring fingerMind
Middle fingerIntelligence
Index fingerIndividual self
ThumbCosmic self

The course describes the body as coming from parents and Earth; the mind as shaped by social conditioning; and intelligence as the capacity to discern beyond inherited opinions. The index finger represents the individual soul (Jeevatma), while the thumb represents the shared cosmic source (Brahmatma).

Its practical point is to recognize conditioning in the mind and limited understanding in the intellect. Within this teaching, meditation lets the individual self turn toward the cosmic self; jnana yoga—the growth of wisdom—is said to arise naturally through dhyana yoga.

02

Age of the Soul

Pages 4–8

Drawing on the Michael Material, the PDF presents seven soul stages. It describes five as participating in physical life and two as transcendent. These labels are part of that spiritual framework, not a way to diagnose, rank or judge people.

01Fragments

The text calls individual souls “fragments” of a universal creative source named the Tao. In its account, a fragment begins a physical cycle and experiences separateness while learning through life.

02“Infant souls”

The source portrays this opening stage as focused on immediate survival, unfamiliar situations and a basic sense of “me” versus “not me.” Its many behavioral stereotypes should be read as claims of the Michael Material, not facts about actual people.

03“Baby souls”

This stage is described as seeking rules, order and a stable community. The PDF says inherited beliefs can feel especially certain here and that opposing views may be unsettling.

04“Young souls”

The course associates this stage with ambition, achievement, experimentation and shaping institutions. It portrays the self as distinct from others and strongly engaged with material goals.

05“Mature souls”

The source emphasizes introspection, relationships, empathy and questions of meaning. It describes a more complex awareness of other people's inner lives and an often-difficult search for truth.

06“Old souls”

The PDF presents this stage as less attached to status or material achievement and more drawn toward spiritual inquiry, synthesis, philosophy and the arts.

07“Transcendental souls”

In the document, this is a largely non-physical stage associated with a sense of union and a teaching role. Claims about embodiment or extraordinary abilities belong to the source's metaphysical account.

08“Infinite souls”

The final stage is described as direct awareness of the Tao or ultimate source. The PDF portrays it as beyond ordinary education or the challenges of physical life.

03

We Are Deathless Soul-Entity Essence

Pages 9–14

Patriji's teaching distinguishes the mortal biological body from an enduring soul-entity described as energy, wisdom and consciousness. Birth is presented as entry into a body and death as departure from it. This is a spiritual interpretation of mortality, not a scientific account of what happens after death.

The PDF uses the Bhagavad Gita, dreams, sleep and a “silver cord” to develop this view. It also sketches a cycle through physical, astral and causal worlds, with repeated lives understood as opportunities for learning. Its descriptions of astral travel, lucid dreaming and out-of-body experience should be understood as course claims rather than required meditation experiences.

The text connects this perspective with living purposefully: learn, practice meditation, study and serve while alive rather than wishing to leave life early. Grief after a death remains a natural human response, whatever a person's spiritual beliefs.

01There Are “Three Deaths”

Within the PDF's model, the first death is leaving the physical body; the second is setting aside an astral body after an astral-world phase; and the third is leaving a causal body as the soul-essence moves to a “supra-causal” world.

The course links that final transition with enlightenment and the letting go of self-importance. These are metaphysical descriptions, not observable stages of clinical death.

04

Law of Karma

Pages 15–19

The course defines karma as action and frames its central principle as “as we sow, so we reap.” It asks readers to consider how thoughts, words and deeds shape consequences for themselves and others, including across lifetimes within its spiritual worldview.

Six laws named in the source

  1. Consciousness: the teaching regards matter, energy and thought as expressions of consciousness.
  2. Incarnation and reincarnation: consciousness is said to enter and re-enter life forms.
  3. Karma: actions are understood to have consequences.
  4. Constant progression: every experience is said to add to learning.
  5. Hastening progression: practices such as meditation, study and service are presented as ways to grow.
  6. Uncertainty: no single event can be fully predicted because many factors interact.

The PDF strongly advocates compassion toward animals, vegetarianism, careful speech and meditation. Its claims that illness is caused by past karma or that meditation replaces doctors, hospitals or medicines are not established medical facts and should not be used to blame people for illness or delay care.

05

The Four Agreements

Pages 19–21

The document presents four practical commitments for communicating and acting with greater awareness.

01Be Impeccable with Your Word

Speak honestly and responsibly. The course asks readers not to use words to shame themselves, blame others or gossip, and to make commitments they intend to keep.

02Don't Take Anything Personally

Other people's comments and actions often reflect their own perspective. Pause before accepting an opinion as a definition of who you are.

03Don't Make Assumptions

Check an interpretation before treating it as fact. Ask questions and clarify expectations instead of building a reaction on a guess.

04Always Do Your Best

Act with care without demanding perfection. The PDF notes that your best changes with circumstances; healthy habits, clear boundaries and steady effort matter more than overexertion.

06

Spiritual Science

Pages 21–22

The PDF borrows the language of cause and effect from science as an analogy for human events: desired outcomes depend on a combination of practices and conditions, not just a wish for results. This analogy is part of the course's spiritual teaching; it is not a tested chemical equation for life outcomes.

Its named “ingredients” are M = meditation, SW = swadhyaya (self-study), SA = sajjana sangatya (good company), A = ahimsa (non-harming), and V = vegetarianism. Sincerity and seriousness are compared with the temperature and pressure needed for a reaction.

The practical takeaway is to bring intention and consistency to these values while distinguishing spiritual metaphors from empirical scientific evidence.
