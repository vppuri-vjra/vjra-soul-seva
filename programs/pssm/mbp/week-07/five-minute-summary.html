010:00–1:00

A Goal Is Direction with a Timeline

We all have wishes, but a goal gives a wish a direction, a measure and a time horizon. Goals are not permanent identities; they can change as we learn and move through different stages of life.

The aim is to live by conscious design rather than repeat yesterday’s patterns by default.

021:00–2:00

Look at the Whole Wheel of Life

The twelve spokes cover experience—relationships, friendship, adventure and environment; growth—health, learning, skills, spirituality and finances; and contribution—creativity, family and community.

Score each area honestly. Balance does not require equal scores; it requires awareness of what needs attention and what must not be sacrificed.

032:00–3:00

Use the Five-Part Framework

  1. Premise: What do I believe here?
  2. Vision: What do I truly want?
  3. Purpose: Why does it matter, and how do I want to feel?
  4. Reflection: Is my present state aligned with that future?
  5. Strategy: What will I visualize, practise and do?
043:00–4:00

Make the Goal Visible and Actionable

Write a SMART goal: Specific, Measurable, Actionable, Realistic and Timely. Use a vision board or mind movie to keep the desired direction visible, and an achievement board to remember what you have already overcome.

Visualization focuses attention. Action produces evidence.

054:00–5:00

Take the Step Only You Can Take

Break the goal down, schedule the first action and review progress. Another person can advise, encourage or hold you accountable, but they cannot do your push-ups for you.

Begin the day aligned with the future rather than pulled immediately into old messages and habits. Learn from mistakes, adjust the plan, and repeat. The deepest result is not only what you obtain—it is who you become.

Five-minute takeaway

Choose from clarity, connect the goal to meaning, and prove the vision through the next consistent action.
