Design a Whole Life,
Not Just a Single Result.

Begin from inner clarity, examine twelve areas of life, and turn a meaningful vision into visible, accountable action.

1 · Overview2 · 5-Minute Summary3 · Themes4 · Executives5 · Grades 8–96 · General Public

A goal is a desired result with a timeline. Goals change as life changes, so the purpose of goal setting is not to freeze life into a rigid plan. It is to give attention, energy and action a conscious direction while remaining present, balanced and willing to learn.

The source material presents goal setting as “life by design rather than by default”: meditation creates clarity, writing makes intention visible, and action turns vision into lived experience.

Experience

How you want life to feel and unfold.

  1. Love and relationships
  2. Friendships
  3. Adventure and new experiences
  4. Environment: home, work, place and lifestyle

Growth

How you want to develop and expand.

  1. Health and fitness
  2. Intellectual life
  3. Skills and meaningful capabilities
  4. Spiritual life
  5. Financial life, career or business

Contribution

What you want to express and give.

  1. Creative life
  2. Family life
  3. Community life and service

Premise

What do I deeply believe in this area? Which belief empowers or limits me?

Vision

What do I genuinely want, stated clearly and with an appropriate time horizon?

Purpose

Why does it matter? What feeling, value or contribution makes it meaningful?

Reflection

Does my present state align with the future I want, or am I acting from fear and lack?

Strategy

What will I visualize, practise and physically do next?

01Prepare the inner state

Meditate Before You Plan

Set aside unhurried time. Begin with natural-breath meditation and allow mental noise to settle. The aim is to write from clarity rather than pressure, comparison or desperation.

Ask: “If no one were judging me, what would feel meaningful, alive and true?”

02See the full landscape

Score the Twelve Spokes Honestly

Give each area a present score from 1 to 10. A low score is information, not failure. Look for imbalance and choose the one or two areas where attention would create the most meaningful change.

Keep the “helicopter view.” Drive and focus are useful, but not when one goal damages health, relationships, integrity or peace.

03Premise

Identify the Belief Beneath the Goal

Beliefs are often our most practised thoughts, shaped by family, culture, society, religion and past experience. Write the automatic sentence that appears in this area: “I never…,” “People always…,” or “I am not….”

Replace an unhelpful belief with one that is believable, empowering and supported by practice. Meditation, right understanding and repeated affirmation can help establish a different response.

04Vision and purpose

Clarify What You Want—and Why

Describe the desired result clearly. Then go beneath the result: What will it allow you to feel, become, learn or contribute? A borrowed goal may look impressive yet leave you unfulfilled.

Make a “feeling list” beside the task list: calm, alive, confident, creative, fulfilled, courageous, loving or useful. The source teaching emphasizes that success without fulfilment is incomplete.

05Reflection

Align the Present State with the Desired Future

Notice whether you are pursuing the goal from inspiration or desperation. Gratitude, appreciation, satisfaction and acceptance of the present moment create a steadier foundation than fear or lack.

You do not attract only what you say you want; your repeated state influences what you notice, choose and sustain.

06Strategy

Visualize, Write and Make It SMART

Write the goal in clear, present-tense language and check that it is Specific, Measurable, Actionable, Realistic and Timely. Make it visible through a goal sheet, vision board or mind movie. Keep a separate achievement board to remember evidence of your own progress.

Visualization is rehearsal, not a substitute for work. Imagine the future experience with feeling, then name the behavior that supports it.

07Execution

Take One Immediate, Visible Step

Break the goal into milestones, monthly priorities and the smallest next action. Put the first step on the calendar. Share it with an accountability partner when that support will help.

Vision without action remains imagination. Vision joined with action becomes commitment.

You cannot pay someone else to do your push-ups and expect your body to become stronger. In the same way, another person cannot perform the consistent actions that create your result.

08Practice and review

Master the Day, Then Repeat

Use the first minutes after waking and the last minutes before sleep to reconnect with the vision. When a useful next step appears, act before habitual hesitation takes over. Consistency matters more than occasional intensity.

Review monthly. Celebrate wins, learn from mistakes, revise goals that no longer fit, and keep room for the unknown. The goal is not only the result; it is also the person you become through the process.

Central practice

Be still. See the whole life. Choose a meaningful direction. Feel it clearly. Write it visibly. Take the next step—and keep becoming.
